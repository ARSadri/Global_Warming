Previous marine biomass burial concepts have been constrained by prohibitive dewatering energy penalties, massive hydraulic transfer volumes, synthetic fertilizer emissions, or unverified open-ocean carbon fate. This revised SPAM-V architecture resolves those bottlenecks through a six-part physical sequence: (1) pumping seawater at ultra-low static head ($\approx 1\text{--}2\text{ m}$) into shallow, plastic-lined (geomembrane) coastal pools adjacent to a deep coastal mine pit; (2) fertilizing the seawater with treated agricultural waste to drive dense microalgal blooms; (3) dosing flocculants directly within the shallow pools to settle the algae in situ; (4) decanting $95\text{--}98\%$ of the clarified supernatant water back to the sea against minimal head ($\approx 1\text{ m}$); (5) gravity-flushing or low-head pumping the remaining concentrated bottom algal sludge into the center of the adjacent deep mine pit; and (6) periodically spreading thin layers of sand over the accumulated benthic mud to drive Terzaghi surcharge consolidation and form laminated, anoxic "engineered turbidites." We contextualize the single-pit and $1,000$-pit global scaling potential against existing CDR pathways and formalize the complete Pool $\times$ Destination state-flux matrix, dimensionally consistent climate-forcing ledger ($\text{tCO}_2\text{e}$), agricultural-carbon attribution rules, and an eight-gate Bayesian falsification sequence.1. Stage 1: Low-Head Seawater Intake into Shallow Coastal Plastic PoolsModular, shallow cultivation pools ($h = 0.30\text{ to }0.50\text{ m}$ depth) are constructed on flat coastal land located immediately between the ocean and the rim of the mine pit. The pools are built using low-cost earth berms lined with heavy-duty, UV-stabilized high-density polyethylene (HDPE) or linear low-density polyethylene (LLDPE) plastic geomembranes. Because the pools sit just above high-tide sea level, axial-flow coastal pumps lift raw filtered seawater against a total static and friction head of only $\Delta H_{\text{in}} \approx 1.0\text{ to }1.5\text{ m}$. Roughly $5\%$ of the previous cycle's settled algal culture is retained in the bottom sump to serve as the living inoculum ($X_0$).Stage 2: Agricultural Waste Nutrient Dosing and Photosynthetic BloomTo avoid the high lifecycle greenhouse gas footprint of synthetic fertilizers, the seawater is dosed with pre-screened, nutrient-rich agricultural waste—such as liquid anaerobic digestate, clarified livestock/poultry manure effluent, or agricultural drainage rich in ammonium ($\text{NH}_4^+$), nitrate ($\text{NO}_3^-$), and orthophosphate ($\text{PO}_4^{3-}$). Over a 14-day active growth phase ($t_g$), solar irradiance and gentle paddlewheel or air-lift circulation drive rapid microalgal division. As photosynthesis consumes dissolved inorganic carbon ($DIC$), pond $pH$ rises ($pH\ 8.5\text{--}9.5$), lowering aqueous $pCO_2$ well below atmospheric equilibrium and drawing atmospheric $\text{CO}_2$ across the shallow water surface.Stage 3: In-Pool Flocculation and Gravity SettlingAt the end of the growth phase (when standing biomass reaches $X_f \approx 0.5\text{ to }1.0\text{ g DW L}^{-1}$), circulation is halted and the shallow plastic pool is dosed directly with a low-toxicity flocculant (e.g., cationic biopolymers, magnesium/calcium hydroxide triggered by high-$pH$ auto-flocculation, or fine mineral coagulant aids). Within $2\text{ to }6\text{ hours}$, the individual microalgal cells aggregate into dense flocs and settle directly onto the smooth plastic floor of the shallow pool, forming a concentrated benthic algal blanket ($\approx 20\text{ to }40\text{ g DW L}^{-1}$, or $2\text{--}4\%$ solids) beneath a layer of clarified seawater.Stage 4: Low-Head Clear-Water Decanting Back to the SeaOnce the algal blanket has settled to the bottom $1\text{ to }2\text{ cm}$ of the pool, floating surface skimmers or side-wall decant weirs draw off the top $95\text{ to }98\%$ of clarified, algae-free seawater and return it directly to the ocean. Because the pool is at coastal elevation, this return flows largely by gravity or requires at most $\approx 1.0\text{ m}$ of low-head pumping to clear coastal outfalls. This step is foundational: by returning greater than $95\%$ of the water directly from the pool back to the sea, the mine pit never receives massive volumes of water, completely preventing pit overflow ("the bathtub problem") and avoiding water-column turbulence ("the blender effect").Stage 5: Concentrated Sludge Transfer Toward the Center of the PitWith the clear water removed, only the thick algal sludge remains on the smooth, slightly sloped plastic floor of the pool. A small volume of flush water or a low-power positive-displacement/slurry pump pushes this concentrated algal paste out of the pool's bottom sump into a transfer pipe leading into the adjacent mine pit. Because the floor of the mine pit lies $100\text{ to }200\text{ m}$ below the elevation of the coastal pools, gravity provides the driving head. The discharge pipe extends along a submerged tether or inclined guide to inject the dense algal slurry directly toward the deepest geometric center of the pit below the chemocline, preventing the sludge from coating shallow pit walls where sunlight or oxygen could degrade it.Stage 6: Deep Benthic Accumulation in the Stratified PitInside the permanently flooded mine pit, the injected algal slurry settles into the central benthic depression. Because only a small volume of dense slurry enters the pit each cycle, the pit's vertical density stratification (an oxygenated upper layer separated by a sharp pycnocline/chemocline from a cold, hypersaline, anoxic bottom layer) remains undisturbed. Any small excess volume of displaced water in the pit is passively bled off from the clean surface layer over a weir or evaporated.Stage 7: Periodic Sand Spreading (Engineered Turbidite Capping)After one or more sludge deposition cycles, a barge, slurry spreader, or pneumatic broadcaster distributes a thin, uniform layer of sand ($1\text{ to }5\text{ cm}$ thick, sourced from coastal silica/carbonate sand or benign mine overburden fines) across the center of the pit. Having a specific gravity of $\approx 2.65$, the sand grains rain rapidly through the water column and settle evenly over the soft algal mud layer. This periodic sand cap performs four critical engineering functions:Surcharge Consolidation: The heavy sand layer acts as a permeable piston, pressing down on the low-density algal mud, expelling trapped porewater upward through the sand pores, and compressing the underlying algae into a dense, low-permeability organic lamina.Physical Anti-Resuspension Shield: The sand forms a heavy armor layer that prevents bottom currents or gas bubbles from resuspending the fine algal particles into the water column.Diffusive Tortuosity Barrier: Alternating layers of compacted algal mud and sand create a laminated sediment stack ("engineered turbidites") that increases diffusive path lengths ($L_{\text{eff}}$), slowing the upward escape of dissolved inorganic carbon ($DIC$), dissolved organic carbon ($DOC$), and dissolved methane ($\text{CH}_4$).Geochemical Bio-Filter: Incorporating iron-bearing sands or carbonate sands provides reactive mineral surfaces and terminal electron acceptors that stimulate Anaerobic Oxidation of Methane (AOM) within the cap matrix before dissolved $\text{CH}_4$ can reach the open water column.Stage 8: Reset and Continuous Carbon-Fate VerificationImmediately after the bottom sludge is evacuated from the coastal plastic pool (retaining a $5\%$ residual volume as inoculum), the pool is refilled with fresh seawater (Stage 1) to initiate the next 14-day bloom. Concurrently, automated sensor arrays in the ponds, decant outfalls, and pit water column log carbon, nutrient, and greenhouse gas fluxes to verify net atmospheric removal.3. Geomechanical and Geochemical Physics of Periodic Sand CappingSpreading a thin layer of sand ($h_{\text{sand}} = 1\text{ to }5\text{ cm}$) over each deposited batch of algal sludge at the center of the pit transforms loose organic floc into a geologically stable sedimentary deposit through three quantifiable mechanisms.4.1 Surcharge-Driven Terzaghi Consolidation and Porewater ExpulsionFreshly settled algal flocs have a high water content ($\phi_0 \approx 0.95\text{--}0.97$) and near-zero effective stress ($\sigma'_v \approx 0$), making them susceptible to resuspension. When quartz or carbonate sand ($\rho_s \approx 2,650\text{ kg m}^{-3}$, bulk submerged unit weight $\gamma'_{\text{sand}} = (1 - \phi_{\text{sand}})(\rho_s - \rho_w)g \approx 9.8\text{ kN m}^{-3}$) is spread over the algal layer, it imposes an immediate vertical effective surcharge stress $\Delta \sigma'_v = \gamma'_{\text{sand}} h_{\text{sand}}$.According to one-dimensional Terzaghi consolidation theory:$$\frac{\partial u_e}{\partial t} = c_v \frac{\partial^2 u_e}{\partial z^2}, \qquad c_v = \frac{k_v}{\rho_w g m_v}$$where $u_e$ is excess porewater pressure, $k_v$ is vertical hydraulic conductivity, and $m_v$ is the coefficient of volume compressibility. Because the sand layer is highly permeable ($k_{v,\text{sand}} \sim 10^{-4}\text{ m s}^{-1}$) relative to the compressing algal mud ($k_{v,\text{mud}} \sim 10^{-8}\text{ m s}^{-1}$), each sand layer acts as a horizontal drainage blanket. Porewater squeezed out of the underlying algal layer drains rapidly into the sand interbeds, decreasing the void ratio $e$ of the algal layer, increasing its shear strength $\tau_f = c' + \sigma'_v \tan \phi'$, and reducing its vertical permeability by orders of magnitude.4.2 Diffusive Tortuosity and Multi-Layer Turbidite TrappingIn a stacked sequence of $N$ alternating algal-mud layers (thickness $d_m$, porosity $\phi_m$) and sand-cap layers (thickness $d_s$, porosity $\phi_s$), upward molecular diffusion of dissolved remineralization products ($DIC, DOC, \text{CH}_4$) is governed by Fick's First Law with effective sediment diffusivity $D_{\text{eff}} = \frac{\phi}{\theta^2} D_0$ (where $\theta^2 \approx 1 - 2\ln(\phi)$ is geometric tortuosity):$$J_{\text{upward}} = - D_{\text{eff,harmonic}} \frac{\Delta C}{\sum_{k=1}^N (d_{m,k} + d_{s,k})}$$As surcharge consolidation compresses $\phi_m$ from $0.96$ down to $0.60\text{--}0.70$, tortuosity increases sharply, trapping dissolved carbon in the deep porewater.4.3 Suppression of Ebullition (Bubble Venting) and Stimulation of AOMThe greatest climate risk in benthic organic storage is methane ebullition—where dissolved $\text{CH}_4$ concentrations exceed hydrostatic solubility ($C_{\text{sat}}(P, T, S)$), forming gas bubbles that punch through the sediment and rise rapidly to the atmosphere, bypassing water-column oxidation.Hydrostatic Solubility Ceiling: At the center of a $180\text{ m}$ deep flooded pit ($P \approx 19\text{ atm}$), methane solubility is $\approx 19\times$ higher than at the surface ($\sim 25\text{--}30\text{ mmol CH}_4\text{ L}^{-1}$), suppressing bubble nucleation.Cap Capillary Entry Pressure: A compacted sand-silt cap provides a mechanical capillary barrier and distributes upward gas migration into slow dissolved diffusion rather than localized bubble conduits.Sulfate- and Iron-Mediated Anaerobic Oxidation of Methane (AOM): By mixing seawater (containing $28\text{ mM SO}_4^{2-}$) and reactive iron/manganese oxide minerals into the sand cap, upward-diffusing $\text{CH}_4$ encounters a permanent Sulfate-Methane Transition Zone (SMTZ) inside the sand cap:$$\text{CH}_4 + \text{SO}_4^{2-} \xrightarrow{\text{ANME + SRB}} \text{HCO}_3^- + \text{HS}^- + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$$$\text{CH}_4 + 8\text{Fe(OH)}_3 + 15\text{H}^+ \longrightarrow \text{HCO}_3^- + 8\text{Fe}^{2+} + 21\text{H}_2\text{O}$$This converts high-GWP methane into alkalinity ($\text{HCO}_3^-$) and precipitates insoluble carbonate and iron-sulfide ($\text{FeS}_2$, pyrite) minerals directly inside the sand cap.5. System Architecture: The State-Flux Matrix and Decoupled Climate Ledger5.1 The Foundational DistinctionThe SPAM-V mathematical spine enforces a strict separation among three physical and accounting quantities:$$\boxed{\text{Vault Storage } (C_{\text{vault}}) \;\neq\; \text{Atmospheric CO}_2\text{ Removal } (\Delta M_{\text{CO}_2}) \;\neq\; \text{Net Climate Impact } (\Delta \mathcal{C}_{H})}$$5.2 The Pool $\times$ Destination State-Flux MatrixEvery carbon atom entering or fixed within the facility is tracked across a non-overlapping state matrix $\mathbf{C}_{i,j}(t)$ $[\text{t C}]$ and directed flux tensor $\mathbf{F}_{ij \rightarrow kl}(t)$ $[\text{t C yr}^{-1}]$:Carbon Chemical Pools ($i, k$):$$i \in \{\text{POC}_{\text{bio}}\text{ (living algae)}, \text{POC}_{\text{det}}\text{ (detritus/sludge)}, \text{POC}_{\text{ag}}\text{ (allochthonous ag-waste C)}, \text{DOC}, \text{DIC}, \text{CH}_4, \text{CO}_2\text{(g)}, \text{PIC}_{\text{mineral}}\}$$Physical Destinations ($j, l$):$$j \in \{\text{pool (coastal plastic pond)}, \text{decant\_sea (coastal outfall)}, \text{vault\_sed (sand-capped pit floor)}, \text{vault\_water (pit water column)}, \text{gw (groundwater)}, \text{atm (atmosphere)}\}$$The conservation differential equation for any state cell $(i,j)$ is:$$\frac{dC_{i,j}}{dt} = \sum_{k,l} F_{kl \rightarrow ij}(t) - \sum_{k,l} F_{ij \rightarrow kl}(t) + R_{i,j}(\mathbf{C}, \mathbf{z}_j, \boldsymbol{\theta})$$where $R_{i,j}$ represents internal biogeochemical phase transformations (photosynthesis, respiration, sulfate reduction, methanogenesis, AOM, and authigenic carbonate precipitation) governed by the local environmental state vector:$$\mathbf{z}_j(t) = [O_2, pH, Eh, T, S, \text{NH}_4^+, \text{NO}_3^-, \text{PO}_4^{3-}, \text{SO}_4^{2-}, \text{H}_2\text{S}, \text{Fe}^{2+}, TA, \mathbf{u}_{\text{flow}}, \phi, \sigma'_v]$$5.3 Dimensionally Consistent Climate Ledger and Sign ConventionsExplicit Sign Convention: Across all equations, $\Delta M_{\text{CO}_2} \gt 0$ and $\Delta \mathcal{C}_H \gt 0$ denote net removal of greenhouse gases from the atmosphere (net climate cooling benefit), whereas negative values denote net warming emissions.1. Counterfactual Atmospheric $\text{CO}_2$ Mass Reduction ($\Delta M_{\text{CO}_2}(t)$, $[\text{t CO}_2]$):$$\Delta M_{\text{CO}_2}(t) = M_{\text{atm,CO}_2}^{\text{CF}}(t) - M_{\text{atm,CO}_2}^{\text{SPAM}}(t) = \frac{44}{12} \int_0^t \left[ F_{C,\text{atm}}^{\text{CF}}(\tau) - F_{C,\text{atm}}^{\text{SPAM}}(\tau) \right] d\tau$$where $F_{C,\text{atm}}$ $[\text{t C yr}^{-1}]$ is the net surface-to-atmosphere carbon flux integrated across the coastal plastic pools, the coastal ocean receiving the decanted water, and the mine pit surface.2. Horizon-Integrated Net Climate Impact ($\Delta \mathcal{C}_{H_{\text{assess}}, H_{\text{GWP}}}$, $[\text{t CO}_2\text{e}]$):
Evaluated at assessment horizon $H_{\text{assess}} \in \{10, 100, 500\text{ yr}\}$ using declared Global Warming Potential horizon $H_{\text{GWP}} \in \{20, 100\text{ yr}\}$:$$\Delta \mathcal{C}_{H_{\text{assess}}, H_{\text{GWP}}} = \Delta M_{\text{CO}_2}(H_{\text{assess}}) - \int_0^{H_{\text{assess}}} \left[ \dot{E}_{\text{process}}(\tau) + \dot{E}_{\text{LC}}(\tau) + \dot{E}_{\text{non-CO}_2, H_{\text{GWP}}}(\tau) \right] d\tau$$where every term is strictly in $\text{t CO}_2\text{e}$:$\dot{E}_{\text{process}}(t)$ $[\text{t CO}_2\text{e yr}^{-1}]$: Emissions from $1\text{ m}$ seawater intake pumping, decant return, paddlewheel mixing, sludge transfer, and sand spreading.$\dot{E}_{\text{LC}}(t)$ $[\text{t CO}_2\text{e yr}^{-1}]$: Amortized embodied emissions of the HDPE/LLDPE plastic pool liners, piping, flocculant chemicals, agricultural waste transport, and sand excavation/delivery.$\dot{E}_{\text{non-CO}_2, H_{\text{GWP}}}(t)$ $[\text{t CO}_2\text{e yr}^{-1}]$: Direct non-$\text{CO}_2$ biogenic gas fluxes escaping to the atmosphere from both the coastal pools (e.g., $\text{N}_2\text{O}$ from agricultural waste nitrogen) and the mine pit surface ($\text{CH}_4$ and $\text{N}_2\text{O}$):$$\dot{E}_{\text{non-CO}_2, H_{\text{GWP}}}(t) = \dot{M}_{\text{CH}_4,\text{atm}}^{\text{emitted}}(t) \cdot \text{GWP}_{\text{CH}_4, H_{\text{GWP}}} + \dot{M}_{\text{N}_2\text{O},\text{atm}}^{\text{emitted}}(t) \cdot \text{GWP}_{\text{N}_2\text{O}, H_{\text{GWP}}}$$6. Master Causal Sequence, Break-Even Productivity, and Bayesian Inference6.1 Partitioned Master Sequence (Direct Facility Measurement vs. Counterfactual Inference)To prevent overclaiming what pit measurements alone prove, the master causal sequence separates the Directly Measured Facility Domain (Domain A) from the Model-Inferred Counterfactual Domain (Domain B):$$\underbrace{P_{C,\text{auto}} \xrightarrow{\eta_H (\text{in-pool floc})} C_{\text{sludge}} \xrightarrow{\eta_D (\text{pit pipe})} C_{\text{deposited}} \xrightarrow{\text{Sand Cap + }^{13}\text{C}} \mathbf{C}_{i,\text{vault}}(t), \mathbf{F}_{\text{vault}\rightarrow \text{water/gw/atm}}(t)}_{\text{Domain A: Directly Measured & Isotopically Constrained Facility Mass Balance}} \;\Longrightarrow\; \underbrace{\Delta F_{\text{air-sea}} \rightarrow \Delta M_{\text{CO}_2}(t) \rightarrow \Delta \mathcal{C}_H}_{\text{Domain B: Counterfactual Model}}$$Why Decanting Clear Water Back to the Sea Helps Domain B: When photosynthesis depletes $DIC$ in the shallow pool and the clarified, $DIC$-depleted, high-$pH$ water is decanted back into the coastal surface ocean (Stage 4), that parcel of seawater retains its carbonate deficit ($\Delta DIC \lt 0$, elevated $[CO_3^{2-}]$) and continues absorbing atmospheric $\text{CO}_2$ across the coastal ocean surface even if gas equilibration inside the shallow pool was incomplete during the 14-day bloom.6.2 Positive-Performance Set and Break-Even Productivity ($P_{C,\text{BE},H}$)Let $\boldsymbol{\Theta} = (\boldsymbol{\theta}, \mathbf{X}, \mathbf{u})$ denote the joint uncertain state comprising biogeochemical/geotechnical parameters $\boldsymbol{\theta}$, ecological community vector $\mathbf{X} = [X_{\text{algae}}, X_{\text{bacteria}}, X_{\text{grazers}}, X_{\text{pathogens}}]$, and seasonal environmental forcing $\mathbf{u}$. The positive-performance set is:$$\mathcal{P}_+(\boldsymbol{\Theta}) = \left\{ P_{C,\text{auto}} : \Delta \mathcal{C}_{H_{\text{assess}}, H_{\text{GWP}}}(P_{C,\text{auto}}, \boldsymbol{\Theta}) \gt 0 \right\}$$The CDR Break-Even Productivity is the infimum of this set:$$P_{C,\text{BE},H}(\boldsymbol{\Theta}) = \inf \mathcal{P}_+(\boldsymbol{\Theta}) \quad \text{if } \mathcal{P}_+(\boldsymbol{\Theta}) \neq \varnothing$$Because pumping energy in the $1\text{ m}$ plastic-pool + in-pond decant architecture is nearly zero and agricultural waste replaces synthetic fertilizer, $P_{C,\text{BE},H}$ is substantially lower than in high-head whole-culture designs—shifting the primary feasibility hurdle from pumping energy to pond ecological stability and sand-capped benthic methane suppression.6.3 Bayesian Posterior PropagationThe final decision metric is the posterior probability distribution of net climate benefit conditioned on all experimental observations $D$:$$p(\Delta \mathcal{C}_H \mid D) = \int p(\Delta \mathcal{C}_H \mid \boldsymbol{\Theta}) \, p(\boldsymbol{\Theta} \mid D) \, d\boldsymbol{\Theta}$$7.
